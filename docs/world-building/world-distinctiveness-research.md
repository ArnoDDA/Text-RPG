# Mirror Sea Realms — Place Identity Design Brief

This is a design brief, not canon. It does not replace any encyclopedia entry.

## What the reference worlds do well

A memorable fantasy place is not defined first by climate or a single crisis. It gives a player an answer to six everyday questions:

1. **What keeps people alive here?** Trade, grain, water, mining, fishing, routes, craft, or a strategic duty.
2. **Who can say yes or no?** A crown, council, household, guild, shrine, military office, clan, or a negotiated assembly.
3. **What does an ordinary day feel like?** Street sound, working hours, social etiquette, food, clothing, and what a visitor must learn quickly.
4. **What is the civic wound?** A loss, compromise, inherited danger, unequal system, or unresolved promise that shaped its institutions.
5. **What is publicly admired, and what is privately necessary?** This creates contradiction without making every place dark.
6. **What kinds of stories naturally happen here?** A city should make some forms of play easy and other forms costly.

Waterdeep works because its wards, commercial life, law, visible ruler, hidden political system, and competing interests give the city many playable layers. Minas Tirith works because its defensive shape, inherited office, sacred dead, and relationship to a larger realm make every height and ritual carry political memory. Baldur’s Gate works as a city setting because its streets are not merely a backdrop: people meet institutions, class, danger, and opportunity at a district scale.

## Diagnosis of the current canon

The nine regions already have strong cores: river monarchy, free port, mountain federation, winter coast, oasis compact, mobile grassland polity, forest compact, sea republic, and neutral strait.

The weakness is structural. Each current entry usually follows the same pattern:

> Geographic introduction → governing groups → a current public dispute → possible player roles.

That produces useful adventure hooks, but it gives every place the same speaking rhythm and makes its people feel like representatives of an issue. The revision should retain the existing histories and replace the repeated frame with distinct **civic identities**.

## Required dossier for each major place

Each nation or major city should receive one first-level encyclopedia page and four to six linked second-level pages:

- **The place at a glance:** the one sentence a resident would use to identify home.
- **Life and built form:** 3–5 named districts, routes, landmarks, and what happens there.
- **Power and permission:** offices, guilds, customary authorities, and the thing each controls.
- **Customs and social thresholds:** hospitality, insults, clothing, time, food, work, and what marks an outsider.
- **The old bargain:** the historic wound or compromise the present city still lives under.
- **Story grammar:** conflicts that arise naturally here, plus what this place is a poor fit for.

Each dossier should include only two active tensions. All other material should be stable background, so the world does not force a global plot onto every campaign.

## Nine distinct civic identities to develop

| Place | Core civic identity | Daily scene to build around | Power structure to sharpen | Natural story grammar |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **王冠城 / 阿维恩** | A river monarchy that survives by making unlike jurisdictions share a river. | Locks, court runners, ferries, bridge markets, petitioners, hospital barges. | Crown clerks vs chartered lordships vs river guilds; make the document itself a form of power. | Inheritance, passage, legal proof, mediation, court access, river crime. |
| **碧帆港 / 洛萨** | A free port where freedom is measured by credit, sponsorship, and who can afford risk. | Shift changes on the docks, insurance counters, sailors’ kitchens, auction houses, multilingual lodging. | Split the council into civic voice, capital, and labour; no group alone controls departure. | Reputation, contracts, false cargo, social ascent, labour disputes, foreign arrival. |
| **卡拉德门 / 石门** | An engineering republic whose deepest virtue is responsibility for shared danger. | Furnace shifts, safety bells, lift queues, repair meals, public inspection boards, subterranean waterways. | Give engineers, mountain holds, residents, and deep-road communities separate vetoes and duties. | Technical investigation, rescue, labour, archaeology, accountability, hard choices. |
| **寒港 / 北湾** | A winter harbor where competence, mutual aid, and weather knowledge confer legitimacy. | Common storehouses, tide boards, boat repair, communal forecasts, winter kitchens, rescue watches. | Let port lords own claims, but give winter councils practical authority during survival season. | Rescue, exploration, family obligation, weather-bound intrigue, shared risk. |
| **月井城 / 萨赫拉** | A water city where public survival depends on visible stewardship and measured generosity. | Canal cleaning, shade markets, water ledgers, night caravans, well etiquette, courtyard medicine. | Distinguish water adjudicators, guide houses, temple stewards, and credit merchants. | Resource ethics, caravan politics, medical work, technical claims, diplomacy, desert travel. |
| **长河集 / 塔尔** | A polity that becomes visible by gathering, then proves its law by moving on. | Seasonal construction, song-based records, horse lines, guest fires, temporary courts, route marking. | Make consent, route custodians, singers, host villages, and elder delegates distinct sources of authority. | Negotiation, kinship, mobility, oral evidence, escort, changing borders. |
| **鹿铃集 / 绿荫** | A woodland commons that treats restraint and passage as forms of ownership. | Seasonal fire, shared clearings, herb exchanges, path signs, quiet periods, steward walks. | Separate village households, forest keepers, old-path authorities, and new settlers. | Environmental mystery, slow trust, local custom, mediation, exploration without conquest. |
| **风帆港 / 海燕群岛** | A maritime republic that believes a person earns standing by returning others safely. | Muster rolls, sail lofts, cartography workshops, public ship news, island messengers, return rites. | Sharpen the difference among island delegates, navy, shipwrights, crews, and expedition investors. | Voyage, recovery, public memory, crew politics, mapping, maritime diplomacy. |
| **双灯城 / 瑟兰** | A neutral strait whose neutrality is a costly service, not an absence of conviction. | Pilots changing ships, inspection queues, witness registries, signal towers, mixed courts, night crossings. | Make the neutral system depend on lighthouse oath, public records, rescue duty, and rotating judges. | Evidence, escort, asylum, espionage, treaty interpretation, border lives. |

## Rules that preserve differentiation

- Do not give every city a council, a guild dispute, a market, and a secret archive. Those elements should recur only where they mean something different.
- A city’s landmark must have a civic job: a bridge collects tax, a shrine records the missing, a canal makes debt visible, a tower regulates passage.
- Give every major place one verb that residents value: **record, insure, repair, endure, share, remember, yield, return, witness**. Use it in institutions and ritual, not as a slogan.
- Give every location two recognisable sensory anchors and one social rule. This makes it usable in narration without repeating a canned description.
- Factions should be built from incompatible duties, not simple good-versus-evil teams.
- Preserve player freedom: a place’s public tension is a source of pressure, never a mandatory campaign plot.

## Recommended next implementation order

1. Agree the civic identity sentence for all nine places.
2. Rewrite the two largest urban hubs first: **王冠城** and **碧帆港**. They will set the standard for district-level density.
3. Write the remaining seven as dossiers using the same navigation, but not the same prose rhythm.
4. Only then refresh character origins, starting locations, and curated starting characters so every selection reflects stable canon.
