# Scene Study Notes

This note records the craft observations behind Secret Fire's narrator rules.
It is not a text corpus and contains no copied literary passages. The game must
not imitate a named author's voice or place protected source text into prompts.

## What was missing from the first library

The earlier examples treated a scene as an accumulation of fantasy nouns:
weather, relic, title, threat. That produces decorative atmosphere but little
narrative movement. Strong fantasy prose makes each paragraph change the
reader's practical understanding of the scene.

## Observed techniques

### Epic and historical fantasy

- Use movement through a place to disclose its scale. A gate, road, meal,
  bridge, or weather change provides the frame; history arrives through the
  way people obey, repair, fear, or misuse it.
- Alternate pressure and release. A long passage of travel or observation has
  a different rhythm from a short decision, a line of dialogue, or a sudden
  constraint. Do not write every scene at a permanently heightened register.
- Keep the point of view inside a person moving through the world. The reader
  learns a landscape because it affects route, shelter, duty, or hope.

### Everyday magic and mystery

- Start with an ordinary procedure the reader can understand: counting,
  shopping, classwork, paperwork, a household task, a guard's routine.
- Make one step behave impossibly, then show the change in physical sequence.
  The strange fact gains force when the next visible object is mundane and
  useful rather than another abstraction.
- A discovery must give the player an investigable question. It does not need
  to explain the world; it needs to change what can be attempted now.

### Folklore, danger, and dialogue

- Let a local fear belong to local life: an inherited rule, a seasonal task, a
  debt, a religious practice, or an account different villagers disagree on.
- Put moral ambiguity in competing needs, not in vague declarations that no
  choice is easy. A farmer, official, hunter, and survivor should each be able
  to name what will be lost.
- Use dialogue to change status. A response should refuse, price, redirect,
  reveal a limit, conceal a fact, or expose a contradiction.

## Operational rules for the game

1. A scene uses one primary grammar—arrival, rest, travel, discovery,
   negotiation, dread, action, ritual, intimacy, or mythic decision.
2. The narrator begins with an observable operation or condition, then creates
   one material change, then leaves a choice or a changed relationship.
3. The system rotates primary grammars when the player action permits it. A
   continuing fight, investigation, or ritual may retain its grammar because
   continuity matters more than novelty.
4. One compact scene rule, not an example passage, is supplied for each turn.
   This preserves token budget and prevents the narrator from copying a fixed
   surface style.
